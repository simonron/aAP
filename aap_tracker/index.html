Adapted Autonomic Profile (aAP) protocol

What does it entail?

Measure blood pressure (BP) and heart rate (HR) at the key times below at home using a personal approved BP/HR monitor. The readings show how the autonomic nervous system responds to postural change, food and exertion.

Record time, position, BP, HR and key symptoms such as dizziness.

  • WAKING — measure lying, after 3 minutes sitting, then after 3 minutes standing.
  • EATING — within 10–15 minutes after breakfast, lunch or dinner, measure lying and after 3 minutes standing; record food and drink, including alcohol.
  • EXERTION — record after 3–5 minutes of physical, cognitive or emotional activity, morning and afternoon, separated from lunch and dinner. Physical exertion may be gentle walking or stairs; emotional exertion may be an exciting match or film; cognitive exertion may be a crossword.

The waking reading is the only routine seated reading. If standing is difficult, substitute sitting, especially after exertion or food. Additional symptom-provoking activities may be recorded with time, event/activity and position.

Does it involve preparation?

Choose a normal day when you can complete the measurements. No change in usual schedule is needed. The aAP can be repeated on another day if required.

What are the advantages?

It informs the clinician about autonomic responses to common daily triggers and may help identify activities that worsen symptoms.

Are there risks?

Standing may cause dizziness or fainting. Lean against a wall when checking standing BP/HR and, if possible, have another person present. Abandon the test and sit or lie down if symptoms worsen.

Discomfort or after-effects?

The BP cuff may feel tight for a short period, particularly with high BP. There should be no after-effects.

Where does it take place?

The test can be undertaken at home, independent of the GP surgery or hospital, and repeated when convenient.

Please bring the record to your appointment and take a picture on your phone as a backup.
